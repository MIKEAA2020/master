Abstract
Can a process with a fixed environmental input be represented naturally as one into another
physical system? Categorically, does tensoring with the environment have a right adjoint,
or causal internal hom? The dimension-profile method introduced here records the number
of independent parameters in each family of processes. Any adjunction preserves convex
mixtures and therefore these counts. The intercept principle turns systematic violations into
geometric or arithmetic no-go tests.
Applied to quantum and classical channels, instruments, comb states, and superchan-
nels, this yields complete or conditional classifications. Quantum channels fail an all-input
arithmetic test; even exceptional dimension matches have incompatible boundary geometry.
Classical channel dimensions match, but products of simplices are not simplices. Nontrivial
instruments fail grade by grade. Adding a fixed channel type admits a right adjoint only
when the added type is trivial. Comb-state dimensions are derived separately from arbitrary
higher-order process spaces.
Dimension is only the first layer. The analysis separates affine shape, positivity with
ancillas, and naturality; constructs the exact linear candidate for a causal internal hom; and
proves that universal evaluators assemble into an adjunction. Finite tomography, symmetry,
and robustness make the converse testable. Extensions treat generalized probabilistic the-
ories, composites without local tomography, positive extension, and finite operator-system
realization.
Keywords: Environmental currying; causal internal hom; dimension-profile obstruction; quantum
channels and superchannels; higher-order quantum processes; generalized probabilistic theories.

The dimension-profile condition is necessary but not sufficient. Deterministic classical channels
exhibit this distinction: transformed dimensions are realizable, but for a nontrivial environment
and codomain the required channel polytope is a nontrivial product of simplices and cannot be
the simplex state space of a classical object. Quantum channels furnish a second separation: some
individual dimensions agree, but the channel body’s nonclosed extreme-point set cannot match
the closed pure-state boundary of a quantum state space. This yields parallel necessary tests of
increasing structural resolution: dimension profile; affine shape and extreme-boundary topology;
ancillary complete-order structure; and naturality and composition. The ordering does not assert
that passing one test implies the next. For compact normalized bases, affine isomorphism already
determines the generated ordinary cone, so affine-base and generated-cone representability are
one test rather than two.
The principal results are as follows.
(i) An ordinary adjunction is automatically affine when TE is affine and composition is separately
affine. The concrete no-go theorems therefore obstruct ordinary, not merely enriched, right
adjoints.
(ii) The dimension-profile theorem is formulated without a preferred basis, and the relation-space
theorem recovers coefficient comparison while retaining all dependencies among chosen
factors.
(iii) The causal internal-hom vector space W(E, Y ) is constructed and shown to give exact
currying of the normalization fibers at the linear level. The tensor-unit state-space test is
separated from affine storage retracts and from full adjoint representability. Objectwise
evaluators with unique factorization for every test object are shown to assemble automatically
into the right-adjoint functor.
(iv) For deterministic quantum channels, the object-dimension equation is completely classified
and its exact arithmetic profile defect is calculated. A nonclosed extreme-boundary theorem
then gives the complete affine classification of channel bodies that can be quantum state
spaces.
(v) Deterministic classical channels show that profile invariance is not sufficient: a vertex-count
mismatch gives a strict polytopal obstruction.

reduced to semialgebraic Choi feasibility.

Proposition 2.3 (Automatic affinity of ordinary transposition). Let C have convex hom-sets and
separately affine composition. Let T : C →C act affinely on every hom-set. If T has an ordinary
right adjoint R, then every adjunction bijection
ΦX,Y : C(TX, Y ) −→C(X, RY )
is an affine isomorphism.
Proof. The inverse transpose is
ΨX,Y (g) = ϵY ◦T(g),
where ϵ is the counit. The functorial action of T is affine, and postcomposition by the fixed map
ϵY is affine. Hence ΨX,Y is affine. It is bijective by the adjunction, so Lemma 2.2 shows that its
inverse ΦX,Y is affine.
Remark 2.4. For quantum channels, classical stochastic maps, and each fixed instrument grade,
ordinary adjunctions therefore induce affine transpositions.

Remark 3.3 (Intercept terminology). Suppose a two-dimensional profile space has linearly
independent coordinates fbulk and fnorm, and every realizable profile has the form
pY = α(Y )fbulk + c fnorm,
c ̸= 0.
The fixed coefficient c, arising from the normalization constraint, is the intercept. If ℓextracts
the fnorm coefficient, then the realizable locus is contained in the affine hyperplane
ℓ(p) = c.
The intercept obstruction is therefore the two-coordinate instance of Corollary 3.2. The dimension-

Remark 3.4 (Necessity, not sufficiency). Profile-locus invariance is a necessary condition. It

an explicit category in which dimensions pass this test but affine representability still fails.

Causal Internal Homs and the State-Space Test
At the linear level, environmental currying is governed by a causal internal-hom space. Its
construction also specifies the structural role of discarding.

5.2
State-space and storage boundary
Three levels are distinguished: an affine storage retract, an affine identification with an available
state space, and unique evaluation for every test object. The first is weaker than the second,
while the all-test-object evaluation property is equivalent to a right adjoint.
Corollary 5.3 (State-space test). If TE = −⊗E has a right adjoint in a monoidal category with
tensor unit I, then
C(E, Y ) ∼= C(I, RY ).
Whenever Proposition 2.3 applies, this is an affine isomorphism. Thus the convex set of E-input
processes to Y must be the state space of an available object.
Proof. The adjunction supplies natural bijections
C(X ⊗E, Y ) ∼= C(X, RY )

for every object X. Taking X = I gives
C(I ⊗E, Y ) ∼= C(I, RY ).

resulting state-space bijection is affine.
Remark 5.4 (Representability boundary). Theorem 5.2 is a statement about the algebraic
tensor product. Its direct application to a physical composite uses local tomography and the
monoidal factorization uX⊗E = uX ⊗uE. Theorem 19.1 below gives the corresponding relation-
and-excess decomposition without local tomography. Environmental currying additionally requires
the resulting normalized base, positive cone, and relevant complete-positivity structure to be
represented by an object of the physical category.
Remark 5.5 (Process-storage reading of the state-space test). A right adjoint to TE = −⊗E
identifies every process space C(E, Y ) affinely with the state space of a single object R(Y ),
naturally in Y . Corollary 5.3 supplies this necessary tensor-unit condition; the quantum and
classical examples below obstruct it by dimension and convex shape, respectively. The condition
does not address arbitrary non-natural encodings, approximate storage, or state-dependent
recovery.

deterministic quantum channels fail condition (i) of Theorem 18.10: for B = E, the causal
internal-hom dimension is not the Hermitian-space dimension of any quantum object. Theorem 6.3
additionally shows that every nontrivial fixed-codomain channel body fails the affine state-space
condition, even when its dimension is compatible. Deterministic classical channels satisfy the
dimension condition but fail the same affine condition because the required process space is a
product of simplices rather than a simplex.
The graded instrument and deterministic-superchannel theorems exhibit analogous failures
outside the specific GPT hypotheses of Theorem 18.10. All instrument grades together fail
through an affine-hyperplane relation, while every nontrivial fixed grade fails the affine state-
space test by Theorems 9.8 and 9.10. Fixed-type superchannel decorations fail through input-
coefficient comparison or, for pure output decoration, the discrete output-dimension spectrum;
their diagonal tensor-unit components are affinely representable exactly when the target output
is one-dimensional. In a symmetric compact-closed category, compact transposition supplies the
representing object and the required naturality. Theorem 15.6 shows that this structure cannot
coexist nontrivially with a terminal tensor unit.

Scope of the Results
The results primarily concern representability of hom-functors and existence of right adjoints;
Theorem 10.4 also records the dimension-regular left-adjoint consequence of the same explicit
profile law. Failure of a right adjoint does not imply that every individual process lacks a left
inverse.
Dimension-profile invariance is necessary but not sufficient for representability. The classical
stochastic vertex obstruction and the quantum extreme-boundary obstruction demonstrate the
need for finer affine invariants when the dimension condition is satisfied.
The autonomous controller theorem is a finite classical-comb construction. Its program values
are perfectly distinguishable classical states, and a desired control is selected by preparation of
the initial program state. It does not assert coherent quantum control, indefinite causal order,
or representable descent. Likewise, a finite matrix-state certificate includes the assertion that
its maps reflect positivity at every matrix level. The certificate theorem constructs a cover from
such certified data; it does not derive the certificate from operator-system dimension or from an
uncertified finite truncation of the matrix order.
The profile and affine-rank defects are geometric quantities. Without a specified operational
norm and continuity assumptions, they do not yield metric error bounds; Proposition 16.6 gives
an explicit finite-dimensional example. The stability estimate in Theorem 13.5 has different
hypotheses: it is relative to a calibrated tester, a positive weight matrix, and the resulting
smallest singular value. Informational completeness on evaluated processes also cannot eliminate
a nontrivial evaluator fiber. The finite response-rank condition is the exact experimental separation
boundary. The symmetry-orbit criteria assume an exact finite or compact group action and closure
of the accessible outcomes under that action. The perturbation corollary permits approximate
implementation only when the actual affine outcomes still factor through the evaluator and
the explicit frame-error threshold holds. The metric transposition theorem separately controls
inverse, naturality, and triangle defects on specified hom-set metrics. It does not supply tensorial
error accumulation, asymptotic regularization, or a noncompact-group theorem without further
Lipschitz and analytic hypotheses.

Conclusion
Environmental currying requires more than a formal reassignment of an environmental wire.
It requires the physical category to represent the causal internal-hom process space. The first
necessary test is invariance of the realizable dimension-profile locus. Quantum channels fail the
all-codomain requirement because, at B = E, the required vector-space dimension is not a square.
At individual dimension-compatible codomains they still fail the affine state-space test because
their extreme-point set is not closed. Classical channels fail the same test by product-of-simplices
vertex counts. Instruments fail globally by a nonzero affine-hyperplane intercept; grade by grade,
every nontrivial quantum and classical process body also fails an affine state-space invariant.
For deterministic superchannels, tensoring by a fixed channel type has a right adjoint exactly
when that type is the tensor unit. Input decoration fails by all-source coefficient comparison,
whereas pure output decoration fails by the arithmetic output-dimension spectrum. At the tensor
unit, polygonal sections show that a diagonal environmental component is an affine state space
exactly when the target output is one-dimensional; the weaker dimension equation has a complete
factor-pair classification. Transfer to larger categories is equivalent to relative representability on
the objects RHIY , a condition strictly weaker than global coreflectivity. Set-valued and convex-
causal presheaf completions always supply ambient right adjoints; the latter retains positive
normalized process bodies and affine mixing pointwise. Nontrivial classical environments and
nonunit superchannel decorations nevertheless force some ambient values outside the representable
101

physical image. For a finite base category, every ambient finite presheaf compiles into a fixed
autonomous classical two-stage comb. A finite program monoid summarizes every control word,
its action quotient is the exact minimal classical program register, and one time-homogeneous
run–halt channel executes the program. Representable descent remains strictly stronger. Even
identical complete-ordered hom-bodies need not assemble naturally. Universal evaluators force
lax module and causal coherence. Operationally, surjective programming plus a full-column-rank
finite tester response is sufficient. The calibrated pseudoinverse recovers all affine program
statistics, exactly the affine dimension many independent probabilities suffice, and its smallest
singular value gives the corresponding noise bound. Finite and compact symmetry closure gives
an exact isotypic criterion for forcing this rank from a restricted seed family, with a tight one-seed
frame in the irreducible case. Haar frames have exact finite weighted orbit quadratures, and a
spectral perturbation bound preserves completeness under approximate implementation. More
generally, metric defects of inverse transposition and naturality control both triangle identities,
while hom-set inradii turn affine-dimension mismatch into sharp reconstruction lower bounds. A
maximally entangled probe followed by spanning binary POVMs provides the finite quantum
tester, while a nontrivial evaluator fiber remains invisible to every evaluated-process experiment.
Recursive comb-state dimensions and superchannel hom-set dimensions are treated under their
respective causal and categorical conventions. For any separately defined arbitrary-order category,
the explicit two-variable profile hypotheses of Theorem 10.4 give the exact conditional right- and
dimension-regular left-adjoint conclusions.
The causal internal-hom space
W(E, Y ) = {G : VE →VY : uY G ∈span(uE)}
exhibits the common linear normalization mechanism. Monoidality of discarding and vector-
space currying identify the corresponding normalization fibers. Environmental currying further
requires the resulting vector space, cone, base, and functorial structure to be represented among
the physical objects.
For a restricted GPT process body P, the exact tensor-unit ordered
space is its homogenization H(P) rather than the maximal cone. Affine-base and generated-
order representability coincide, whereas matrix transposition shows that ancillary complete
order is a genuinely stronger property. For concrete matrix realizations, that entire ancillary
tower is decided at the finite level max{m, n}; quantifying over the representing map is a finite
semialgebraic problem with Choi witnesses. At the abstract level, the C∗-envelope gives the
exact finite-realizability criterion, minimal matrix size, and block-unitary classification. A finite
matrix-state certificate constructs a cover and has minimum total size equal to the minimal
embedding size; the cover then computes the Shilov ideal by finitely many complete-order tests.
Three-dimensional examples exclude every dimension-only embedding bound. For a composite
without local tomography, local relations and global quotient degrees modify causal currying by
explicit rank–nullity terms. Positive globalization is governed in general by the quotient image
of the positive-map cone, with exact dual certificates when that image is closed, a transverse
sufficient condition, and robustness under strict feasibility. For nice dual cones, one minimal
face yields equivalent feasible-direction and complementary-face tests. For general semialgebraic
cones, closedness is decidable; nonclosed images admit weak-feasibility curves whose minimal lift
norms diverge at a rational power rate. Extension is a finite LP in polyhedral theories and a
finite Choi SDP in quantum theory, and can fail even for a completely positive local identification.
At the tensor-unit level, an affine storage retract is weaker than a state-space isomorphism, and
a state-space isomorphism is weaker than an all-test-object adjunction. Once an evaluator has
unique factorization for every test object, however, those objectwise universal data assemble
uniquely into the adjunction. The grade-by-grade and superchannel tensor-unit results make
these distinctions explicit in the concrete categories.
The representability hierarchy consists of dimension profiles; affine shape and extreme-
boundary topology; ancillary complete-order structure; and categorical naturality. Ordinary
102

generated-cone representability coincides with affine-base representability under the compact-base
hypotheses above. The channel, instrument, superchannel, and compact-closure results instantiate
these distinct necessary tests. The Bell completely positive adjunction shows in particular that
ambient compact currying may exist while deterministic restriction fails simultaneously at its
trace-scaling unit and rank-deficient counit; the reciprocal-weight calculation also gives the sharp
1 −δ−2 triangle defect under trace-nonincreasing weighted caps. Conversely, Petz maps, Bayesian
inverses, and entropy-increasing or entropy-decreasing dynamics can exist in categories whose
environmental functor has no right adjoint. Dimension is therefore the first representability
invariant, not the last.
